Overview

This is a design exercise, not a coding exercise. You will read a scenario and a component team's architecture proposal submitted to the Architecture Review Board (ARB), then write a structured ARB Review Memo responding to the proposal.

The expected effort is approximately 1.5 hours. If you reach the time limit before finishing, note what you didn't get to and submit what you have — we will not hold an incomplete submission against you.

Please submit at least 4 hours before your scheduled review session.

You may send questions to your interviewer at any point before the session. We will answer within the same business day. Requesting information you do not have is a normal part of this exercise and is not counted against you.

Background: The Enterprise Platform

You are the Cloud Data Architect for the Agency for Health Data Analytics (AHDA), a federal health agency operating an enterprise cloud data and analytics platform hosted on Azure Government. The platform provides governed data storage, Databricks and Azure Synapse Analytics compute, and policy controls for data practitioners across the agency.

Medallion Lakehouse Architecture

The platform uses Azure Data Lake Storage Gen2 organized into three tiers:

Tier Purpose Data Classification Ceiling
Bronze Raw ingest — untransformed source data CUI / PHI
Silver Cleansed and conformed — PII tokenized or removed CUI
Gold Curated, analytics-ready — no raw PII or PHI CUI / Low

All Databricks workloads are governed through Unity Catalog for lineage tracking, access control, and audit. Analyst-facing tables are served exclusively from Gold-tier assets registered in Unity Catalog.

Identity and Zero Trust

Identity follows Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) principles enterprise-wide: least privilege, workload identity federation, and attribute-based access control (ABAC) at the data tier.

ATO and Change Management

The platform holds an Authority to Operate (ATO) under the NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF). Any change that could affect the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of the system — including the introduction of new data sources — requires a Security Impact Analysis (SIA) prior to implementation, reviewed through the platform's change intake workflow.

Architecture Review Board

The ARB reviews all proposals to onboard component platforms or add major capabilities. You chair the ARB.

The Proposal: MCH Division Analytics Platform

The Maternal and Child Health (MCH) Division has submitted the following proposal requesting ARB approval to onboard a new analytics platform as a component platform within the enterprise.

1. Executive Summary

The MCH Division requests ARB approval to onboard a new analytics platform as a component platform within the enterprise data and analytics environment. The platform will enable MCH analysts to access longitudinal health records for program participants and generate actionable insights to support program delivery and reporting obligations.

2. Background and Motivation

The MCH Division currently generates reports by running manual SQL queries directly against its operational case management database (CMS), hosted on-premises at the agency's co-located data center. This approach creates performance pressure on the operational system, produces inconsistent results across teams, and prevents adoption of modern analytics tooling.

The proposed platform will:

  • Decouple analytics workloads from the operational system
  • Provide a stable, versioned copy of program data for analysis
  • Enable self-service analytics for approximately 15 analysts across four MCH program offices

3. Data Description

The MCH CMS contains longitudinal health records for approximately 2.4 million program participants. Records include:

Data Element Classification Notes
Participant demographics (name, DOB, SSN) PHI / CUI Covered under HIPAA Business Associate Agreement
Health encounter records PHI / CUI —
Program enrollment and status CUI —
Aggregate outcome metrics Unclassified Derived; no PII

4. Proposed Architecture

4.1 Ingestion

An Azure Data Factory (ADF) pipeline will extract data from the MCH CMS using a JDBC connection over the existing site-to-site VPN between the co-located data center and the agency's Azure Government landing zone. Full load on the initial run; incremental (changed-data) loads nightly thereafter.

Extracted records will land in Delta Lake tables in the MCH workspace under the mch_gold schema, registered in the MCH Databricks workspace's default Hive catalog.

4.2 Storage

Data will be stored in Azure Data Lake Storage Gen2 in the mch-prod container within the enterprise storage account. Because MCH analysts require access to full participant records for case-level reporting — including demographics and encounter histories — extracted records will be written directly to the Gold tier of the enterprise data lake.

4.3 Compute — Databricks Workspace

The MCH Division will operate a dedicated Databricks workspace (dbx-mch-prod) provisioned within the MCH Azure resource group (rg-mch-prod). The workspace will authenticate to Azure resources using a service principal (sp-mch-databricks) granted Contributor role on rg-mch-prod. The service principal client secret will be stored as a pipeline variable in the MCH Azure DevOps project for use in ADF and notebook-triggered jobs.

4.4 Compute — Analytics Query Layer

A dedicated SQL Warehouse (Always On, XL, 16 DBUs) will serve Power BI Direct Query connections for MCH analysts. The warehouse will remain active at all times to eliminate cold-start latency for the analyst population.

4.5 Data Catalog and Documentation

The MCH team will maintain a data dictionary in a SharePoint list documenting table schemas, field definitions, and data sources. This will serve as the authoritative metadata reference for MCH analytics assets.

4.6 Identity and Access

Analyst access to MCH dashboards will be managed through MCH's existing Active Directory security groups, enforced at the Power BI workspace level. No additional Databricks-level access controls are planned at this time, as all 15 analysts share equivalent data access needs and operate under the same BAA.

5. Network Connectivity

The ADF pipeline will connect to the on-premises CMS using the existing site-to-site VPN. The team has confirmed with network operations that the VPN is already in place and functional. No new network infrastructure, firewall rule changes, or additional routing configurations are anticipated.

6. Compliance and Security

The proposed platform will operate within the existing ATO boundary. No new FedRAMP-authorized services beyond those already in the platform's SSP are being introduced. The MCH CMS already carries its own system boundary and SSP documentation at the agency.

Data classification and handling will comply with the agency's CUI policy. PHI handling will remain under the existing BAA with the program's healthcare partners.

Your Task — ARB Review Memo

Write a structured ARB Review Memo using the template provided in the repository (arb-memo-template.md). Your memo must:

  • State your recommendation: Approve, Approve with Conditions, or Defer
  • Identify every policy or design issue you find in the proposal
  • For each issue: name the problem, cite the relevant policy or principle it violates, and state precisely what the team must resolve before implementation
  • Be specific enough that the MCH team can act on your feedback without a follow-up meeting

Structure each issue as:

Severity — Critical / Major / Minor
Policy / Principle Violated — The rule, standard, or architectural principle this violates
Problem — What is wrong and why it matters
Required Resolution — What the MCH team must specifically do before this proposal can be approved
The proposal above is realistic but incomplete in some areas deliberately, to allow for solution flexibility. Where information is missing, either ask us or record the assumption you made and proceed.
